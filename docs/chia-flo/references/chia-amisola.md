# Chia Amisola

**Who:** Artist, designer, technologist (pronouns: they/them). Based between New York and Manila. Senior Product Designer at Figma; previously Spotify (design systems), Kumu, Rappler. BA in Computing & the Arts, Yale (2022). Founder and director of Developh (since 2016) and the Philippine Internet Archive. Forbes 30 Under 30 Asia; Lumen Prize winner. Has shown at the V&A, Tate Britain, Gray Area, Transmediale, panke.gallery, CultureHub and Tai Kwun.

**Self-description:** "devoted to the internet's loss, love, labor, and liberation, particularly of the third world experience." Makes "work about intimacy, infrastructure, and infinity across text, websites, and performances."

**The line to hold onto:** *"Mostly, they want to gather all the people they love in one place, and build an internet that could be that place."* (everythingi.love/info)

---

## Themes

- **Love as stewardship.** "I read love as a condition and practice of stewarding in each other something that might outlast us both." Websites are love letters: *I love it when you love me too* cycles through phrases from Notes-app moments about a partner.
- **Repetition and ritual, domestic and divine.** The rosary is infrastructure; the women in Chia's family pray for hours, and "what I see in their devotion is similar to what I see in computers." Tallying is computation.
- **Infrastructure as intimate.** Pisonets (coin-operated internet), sachets, air conditioners, data centers, seafarers. Infrastructure is felt, not abstract.
- **Weather, intermittency, slowness.** "The computer is only 'on' when the sun is, not by choice but by circumstance." Scarcity's "necessary roughness": "simply needing less."
- **Containers.** Mary as a living tabernacle; the website as container; "technology and religion… are both containers in which I can place faith."
- **Home.** From Bachelard: "A house that has been experienced is not an inert box. Inhabited space transcends geometrical space." And: "I know that wherever place I end up in, I will make it home."
- **Naming as world-making.** "Much of the making of a world is simply giving a name to it." Domains are "invitations rather than tombstones."
- **Visibility for the unseen.** Third-world and feminized labor, apparitions as glitches that "materialize towards those who lack power."
- **A childhood geography.** "Growing up in Metro Manila, my world was lived between a bedroom, church, and shopping mall."

## Methods

1. **Write inside code.** "I always write within code and contain my words in data structures. I arrange text into arrays, and… the very arbitrary sizing of my editor and its word wrapping affect my enjambment." Text is composed with its presentation algorithm in mind: *reversed, shuffled, patternized, sequenced, bound to time or clicks, waiting, or decaying.*
2. **Metadata as a second voice.** "Secondary metadata like title attributes and tab names reveal narratives." Tab titles, `title=`, alt text and filenames are part of the poem.
3. **The computer doesn't disappear.** Expose the browser, the windows, the OS. "The gap between our body and the computer does [disappear]." Windows arranged by hand into a landscape.
4. **Sites that aren't always awake.** *Cloudwatching* opens only by day and *Stargazing* only by night; other sites expire, are hard to find, or have no owner. Time as a constraint that creates meaning.
5. **One word at a time.** *A Bedroom in Las Piñas* is a black void, a thin hairline frame, and a single word per click ("There", then "Listen"), in a typewriter face and a script italic. Place-titles like stations of a journey: *A Familiar Place, A Catholic Schoolyard, An Empty Overpass, A Child's Bedroom, A Momentary Room.*
6. **Atmosphere layers.** Stacked, low-opacity, slow:
   - `fog`: a drifting texture at about 0.4 opacity on a 190-second linear loop
   - `light`: a blurred white band (`blur(30px)`, `mix-blend-mode: lighten`, about 0.3 opacity)
   - `plasticstars`: glow-in-the-dark stars from a childhood ceiling
   - `scanline`, `halftone` (`color-burn`), `flash-red` / `flash-white` (a 0.5-second one-shot)
   - Randomness as material: `chance(prob)`, `pick(arr)`, `randInt()`
7. **Durational performance.** Reading, clicking, sounding, looping and lagging the browser live. "Performance is inherently a hypertextual practice."
8. **Rituals of entry.** "What if the ritual of human gathering and celebration around the website happens not when you hit publish but every single time someone enters it?"
9. **Care in description.** Images carry bracketed descriptions: "[A near translucent red-purple flower petal that almost looks like a butterfly wing, with a clear image of Mary on the left side.]"

## Visual signature

- **Home (everythingi.love):** a soft, horizontally blurred rainbow or prism field on white. A tiny serif nav (Index · Forms · Info · Explore · News) spread across the top. The index slides in as a drawer and dims the world behind it.
- **Index structure:** works grouped as *(Worlds)*, *(Narrative)* and *(Systems)*, with group names in parentheses. Unfinished works are marked *(WIP)* in a lighter grey. Titles are bilingual: *Paru-paro (Butterfly)*, *PAGASA (Hope)*, *Himala (Miracle)*.
- **Title cards:** a centered serif title with a paired-noun litany beneath: *"Catholicism and cyberfeminism, apparitions and angels, delusion and devotion."*
- **Color:** mostly black, white or paper, with prismatic washes. A soft pink (`#e397b7`) for script headings in *Bedrooms*.
- **Type:** typewriter or monospace-adjacent faces ("Director") paired with a script (Altesse); small serifs for navigation.
- **Glyphs as controls:** ✝ (a cross) and ◑ (a half-moon theme toggle) in the corners, at about 0.5 opacity.

## Voice

- Short declaratives with concrete images: *"We know all the storms by name. We play with our lives."*
- Litanies and alliteration: *love, labor, loss, & liberation*; *intimacy, infrastructure, and infinity*.
- Earnest and confessional (blog): *"Am I the last person on earth who believes in myself?"*
- Political consciousness without slogans: names the conditions (colonial air conditioning, sachet economies) and doesn't moralize at individuals.
- Tagalog with English glosses: *KAKAKOMPYUTER MO YAN! (That's what you get for using the computer!)*

## How Chia would critique a design

- "Who is this *for*, specifically — a name, not a persona?"
- "What happens every time someone enters?"
- "What does it do at night? In a storm? When nobody's here?"
- "Where are the words? Are they yours?"
- "What's the archive? What survives us?"
- "Is the computer hiding, and should it?"
