# Rama Duwaji — the hand, and the hand moving

Rama Duwaji is a Syrian-American illustrator, animator and ceramicist based in Brooklyn. She works in drawn portraiture and movement, with a focus on sisterhood and communal experience. Clients include the Washington Post, BBC, Apple, Spotify, VICE and Tate Modern.

Use her when the work needs **a hand in it**: when an interface is correct but has no touch, when still things need a personality, or when the craft should feel made rather than rendered. Chia gives the soul and Flo the frame; Rama gives the **mark-making and the loop**.

## What her site actually does (looked at live, Oct 2026)

- **Everything is drawn.** Her name is hand-lettered in Arabic as the header. The navigation (ILLUSTRATION, ANIMATION, ABOUT) is hand-lettered in uneven caps, and each nav item is a looping GIF, so the interface itself is animated drawing.
- **One ink.** Black line on white, with pencil grain left in. No UI chrome, no colour unless the drawing brings it.
- **A scene, not a hero.** A woman lies in the grass reading, a book open face-down beside her. Grass and wildflowers frame her, and a black band of soil with white roots runs underneath. The page has a ground, literally.
- **Plain structure.** Three links, a gallery. The richness is in the drawings, not the layout.

## How she talks about the work (Hyperallergic, "In the Studio With Rama Duwaji")

- Limits are generative: *"the best work comes from limitations."* She works from cheap and found materials: cardboard moving boxes, container tops for printmaking, estate-sale scrap wood, secondhand paper, oil pastel, ink, leftover clay.
- Illustration is a puzzle: tell a story or an emotion in one frame, in her own visual language.
- The work is an archive of her life: news, protests, friendships, the architecture around her.
- Ceramics, about five years in, pushes her *"beyond the usual 2D of illustration."* Her favourite space is *"the intersection of ceramics, illustration, and animation."*
- Animation gives humour and movement to things that are usually still.

## Rules to take into interfaces

1. **Draw the interface's own words.** Hand-letter the few words that matter (a name, the nav, axis labels) from the person's real handwriting, scanned and vectorised. Never fake it with a script font. This one move does more than any typeface choice.
2. **Boil the line.** Hand-drawn animation stays alive by cycling three or so slightly different redrawings of the same thing at about 6–8 fps. Use it on small drawn elements (a mark, an object, a nav word) so they breathe, with crisp lines whose paths differ, not a displacement filter. Respect reduced motion by showing one frame.
3. **Give still things a small loop of personality.** A bowl that wobbles, a figure that blinks, grass that sways. One loop per object, slow, and never on text people need to read.
4. **Let the material show.** Pencil grain, a scanned edge, the white soles of an unglazed foot. Texture goes where the hand was, not on every surface.
5. **Put the page on a ground.** A floor, a soil line, a table: something the drawings stand on, so the page feels like a place.
6. **Work within a limit, on purpose.** One ink, one material, one loop. Name the limit in the pass.
7. **The archive is the subject.** The person's real life (friends, the street, the news) is the content. Draw from it; don't decorate around it.

## Where Rama meets Chia and Flo

- **With Chia:** both treat the work as a love letter and an archive of a life. Rama makes it physical (clay, cardboard, ink); Chia makes it networked.
- **With Flo:** both are restrained in structure. Rama's site has three links. The restraint is what lets the hand show.
- **The tension:** Flo wants motion to serve meaning and stay under 200ms for interaction. Rama's loops are ambient and continuous. Resolve it by keeping interaction feedback fast (Flo) and letting only *objects* boil and loop, slowly, never the controls (Rama).

## Lens to add to a critique

| Lens | Question |
|---|---|
